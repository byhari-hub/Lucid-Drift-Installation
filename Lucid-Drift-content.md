# LUCID DRIFT
*An archive that refuses its own completeness*

---
"A room without light.
Only sound—fragments of a melody repeating, unfinished.
Before I understood images, I understood space this way: as something felt, constructed through absence, memory, and rhythm."

A research-driven installation investigating how formally complete archives misrepresent themselves to their inheritors.

An archive can be formally complete and still fail to hold what it was meant to preserve. This is not a statement about loss through neglect or technological failure. It is a statement about the structural inadequacy of formal capture itself.

The project works through this paradox using inherited material — a composer's archive — as both source and site. The work is not a memorial. It is an apparatus.

Using computational design not to extend assumptions about preservation, but to make their failure perceptible — turning the tools of archive against their own claims.

*Lucid Drift* stages the gap between what an archive captures and what a person actually inherits from it.

## Phase 01 — Foundations

### 1.1 Project Intent

*Lucid Drift* examines a specific paradox: an archive can be formally complete and still fail to hold what it was meant to preserve. It works through this paradox using inherited material — the artist's father's compositional archive — as both source and site of investigation.

The work is not a memorial. It is an apparatus for staging the gap between what an archive captures and what a person actually inherited from it.

---

### 1.2 Origin

Hari Tris is a multidisciplinary artist born in Vietnam and based in Singapore. Her encounters with creative processes came through her father's music. She grew up drifting to sleep amid her beloved father's midnight composing sessions — old Vietnamese love songs that painted silent scenes before she had words for them.

These unfinished melodies, looping choruses, and fragments of sound sessions painted silent scenes in her mind long before she had language for them, unfolding like films inside her head. After his passing when she was seven, that process remained — building environments internally, without images, as a way of holding onto something intangible.

Years later, she returned to his work and found it complete. A twelve-track album. A published music book. Digital files. Nothing was lost. Every note had a place. And yet, the way the music lived in her was nothing like the book.

> "The way the music lived in her was nothing like the book."

---

### 1.3 Source Material & Cultural Lineage

The source archive is the recorded and notated work of Nguyên Thanh Tịnh (1950–2004), a Vietnamese composer working within the lineage of *Tân Nhạc* — a 20th-century musical form that emerged from contact between Vietnamese traditional sensibilities, French chanson, and early modern recording technology. The form is hybrid by origin; the archive inherits that hybridity.

The archive exists in three formally complete instantiations: a twelve-track recorded album, a published music book containing every lyric and every score, and digital files of each composition. The record is preserved, reproducible, and accessible — fixed both on the page and in machine-readable form.

The post-2004 inheritance of such an archive by a diasporic daughter raises questions about who holds memory, in what language, across what distance — questions the project takes as part of its material rather than its setting.

### 1.4 Why This, Why Now

Contemporary digital culture operates on three default assumptions about memory: that digitization preserves, that compression is neutral, and that reproduction equals access. Each assumption is structurally flawed, but rarely confronted in the medium that produces it.

*Lucid Drift* uses computational design not to extend these assumptions but to make their failure perceptible — turning the tools of preservation against their own claims.

## Phase 02 — Concept

### 2.1 Conceptual Framework

The project's primary theoretical interlocutor is Diana Taylor's distinction between *archive* and *repertoire* — the archive as the domain of durable, reproducible documents, and the repertoire as the domain of embodied, ephemeral, performed knowledge. Taylor argues that these two modes are categorically different and that the cultural privileging of the archive over the repertoire is itself a form of violence.

*Lucid Drift* takes Taylor's distinction seriously and works in productive tension with it: the project stages the *attempt* to extract repertoire-effects from archival material, and stages that attempt as necessarily failed.

The project further locates its subject-position through Marianne Hirsch's concept of *postmemory* — the relation of a generation to inherited experiences it did not directly live, mediated through fragments, objects, and the testimony of others.

### 2.2 Orphaned Archives

The project operates under a structural concept it names *Orphaned Archives*, defined operationally:

> An archive is orphaned when its preserved content can no longer be activated by anyone with first-person access to the conditions of its production.

This distinguishes orphaning from three adjacent archival conditions. An archive can be *incomplete* (material is missing), *inaccessible* (material is unreachable), or *neglected* (material is unused) without being orphaned. Orphaning is none of these.

Where an incomplete archive declares its own gaps, a complete archive presents itself as sufficient and conceals what it cannot hold — the room, the duration, the unrecorded decision, the listener who was there. The structural claim of the project is that this concealment is the operation worth making perceptible.

> Formal completeness in an archive does not eliminate loss; it conceals it.

---

### 2.3 Core Concept

*Lucid Drift* constructs an environment around a single asymmetrical relation: an archive that is formally complete, and a position of inheritance whose experiential remainder cannot be re-derived from it.

The work does not stage a binary between two memory systems. It stages an encounter — between the archive (external, material, reproducible) and a position of inheritance (internal, embodied, non-reproducible). The asymmetry is the point.

The visitor's role is not interpretive and not performative. It is operational: they perform actions on the archive, and the system responds in ways that make the archive's inability to become what the inheritor remembers perceptible.

## Phase 03 — System

### 3.1 Operative Grammar

The installation produces its conceptual claim through three mechanisms. These are the operative grammar of the work — what distinguishes *Lucid Drift* from a memory-themed sound installation, and what makes the project's argument something the visitor encounters rather than reads.

**Resistance**
When the visitor's actions move toward coherence — toward making the material settle, complete, or play — the system fragments it further. The archive refuses to perform repertoire. This refusal is the work's encounter with Taylor's distinction.

**Inherited Constraint**
Parameters within the system are set by traces of the artist's relation to the material — annotations, durations, silences, refusals — which the visitor cannot see and cannot override. The inheritance is present as the *shape of what is permitted*, not as content.

**Irreversibility**
Each interaction permanently alters the state the next visitor encounters. Nothing is preserved. The installation enacts the impossibility of preservation by being destroyed in use. This is the work's most direct refusal of platform logic.

### 3.2 Interaction & Spatial Design

The installation operates through a tactile control surface that combines instrumental and editorial gestures — the gesture of playing and the gesture of cutting. The interface is intentionally hybrid, reflecting the dual condition of the source material: once embodied and performed, now processed and reproducible.

The visitor encounters the work alone, for a short duration. The environment is calibrated to a threshold register — low light, spatialized sound, a slowed sense of time — but this register is the consequence of the system's behavior, not its decoration. The room is dark because the archive is being asked to carry what it was not made to carry, and that asking takes time.

What the visitor leaves with is not an experience of the artist's memory and not a reconstruction of the father's music. It is the residue of a brief encounter with the gap between them.

### 3.3 Technical Architecture

The work is structured in three computational layers, each developed as an independent investigation. The relations between them are the central design question.

**Audio — Granular Synthesis Engine**
Granular synthesis and time-based processing operate directly on the source recordings. The material is treated as physical substance rather than as data to be regenerated. Visitors can stretch, fragment, and spatialize the recordings, but cannot complete them. Unfinished phrases remain unfinished. This choice is conceptual, not aesthetic.

**Visual — Small Generative Model**
A small generative model trained exclusively on the artist's peripheral archive — childhood recordings, photographs, the music book itself. The model is deliberately under-resourced. Its role is not to produce dreamlike imagery but to perform, in real time, the over-archive's tendency to fill in what cannot be filled in. Its hallucinations are the argument.

**State — Stateless Control Layer**
A control layer connects tactile input to operations across audio and visual domains, with no persistent state. The system holds no memory of itself. Each session degrades the next.

### 3.5 Open Questions

The project does not treat the following as problems to be solved before proceeding. It treats them as material the work continues to think through — the live edge of the research, reflected in the installation rather than smoothed over by it.

**Q—01: Authority over inherited material**
Who holds the right to recompose a deceased composer's work, and how is that authority made visible to the visitor rather than concealed by the smoothness of the system. The project does not resolve this. It makes the question part of what the visitor encounters.

**Q—02: Resistance to aestheticization**
The risk that the work, in a Western exhibition context, is read as exotic or sentimentally beautiful — Vietnamese music, dead father, soft light, drift. The project's defense is structural rather than rhetorical: the operative grammar of resistance, constraint, and irreversibility is designed to prevent the work from settling into the genre it adjacents.

**Q—03: The status of the visitor**
What it means to invite a stranger into a brief, non-preserved interaction with another person's grief-material — and whether brevity and non-preservation constitute sufficient ethical structure or require further articulation. This question remains open in the work, and is itself part of the work.

> These questions are not loose ends. They are the live edge of the research.
